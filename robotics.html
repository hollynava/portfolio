The problem

Running the machine lived in people's heads

ICON's large-scale 3D-printing robots build homes and infrastructure, and when I joined, the operator interface couldn't keep up: clunky, hard to navigate, and nearly unreadable in real field conditions, outdoors, in glare, wearing gloves.

Developers were making UX calls without design input, and the expertise to run a print lived in people's heads and binders. In practice that meant an ICON engineer had to be on site for every build, a hard ceiling on scaling to real customers.

What I did

Turned expert-only operation into a guided flow

Ran deep field research

I watched operators work in real conditions to find where visibility, contrast, gloves, and cognitive load actually broke the experience, rather than designing from a comfortable desk.

Rebuilt the interface for the jobsite

High-contrast visuals, larger hit targets, simplified navigation, and a large-format variant of the design system, built for glare and gloves instead of an office monitor.

Designed a guided, task-based operation system

I partnered with engineering to turn an early concept, automated task-graph traversal of a building design, into a human-operable flow that walks operators step by step instead of relying on memory. An AI-to-human translation layer.

Closed dangerous gaps

I added automated material-refill and cleaning alerts that were easy to miss and could each halt a build for 30-plus minutes, turning silent failures into clear, timely prompts.

Gave leaders visibility

The first real-time view for project and construction managers into what's happening in the field, with productivity and performance metrics they'd never had before.

Designed for what's next

Automation and operator-as-monitor models, plus an early Fleet platform for remote configuration, monitoring, and diagnostics, the groundwork for one person running more than one machine.

Outcome

Operable by anyone, without dumbing down the machine

The guided system shipped roughly 60% of the full build sequence as guided tasks, built to bring operators to independence in weeks instead of months, work that previously required an ICON engineer on site. Managers got their first real-time field visibility, and the system laid the foundation for multi-machine operation and external customer deployment.
