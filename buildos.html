ICON · 3D-printed robotic homebuilding

BuildOS — a product ecosystem for 3D-printed robotic construction

Product StrategySystems Design0→1

ICON isn't just building robots, it's inventing a new way to build, where design, hardware, materials, and services all come together at once. The software to tie those pieces into one experience didn't yet exist. The process spanned a mix of internal and external tools that didn't share data or a common experience and an entire stretch of the journey, from finishing a design to planning the build, had no tooling.

The target customer was also ambiguous, with developers, builders, architects, and homeowners each with very different needs. I identified a single unified ecosystem, built on a common data spine, and owned the strategy and design that made it real: BuildOS, the digital thread that carries a customer from design through construction.

Audited the existing landscape

Mapped every internal and external tool across the design-to-build process, and where friction, duplication, gaps, and scale risk actually lived, working with user groups to ground it in real workflows.

Modeled a shared data spine

Modeled the end-to-end workflow, from design to planning to print to sale to ownership, into a single data spine every module reads from and writes to, so the system could be automated and measured instead of stitched together by hand.

Defined the missing middle

The biggest gap wasn't a weak feature, it was an absent one. I defined a new module for site planning, scheduling, and construction management, the connective tissue between the design and print workflows.

Authored strategy, roadmap, and PRDs

Wrote the ecosystem strategy, an 18-month phased roadmap, and the module PRDs: problem framing, OKRs, scope tradeoffs, and cross-functional alignment — mapped to one connected product ecosystem.

Pressure-tested with customers

Drove a 27-interview research program across developers, builders, general contractors, and specialty trades, then sharpened the modules and priorities from what came back.

Set direction and designed the details

Owned the ecosystem strategy and personally designed the Print operator app end to end, plus concepts for the other modules, so the vision and the shipped screens never drifted apart — one connected set of modules on a shared data spine.

Adopted as the company's product direction

The vision and architecture was adopted as ICON's official product direction. The modules are now in active use, at varying stages of completeness, with customer-facing release scoped to ICON's Titan external launch, targeted for Q1 2027.

The bet underneath it: in a business where the full experience only appears when hardware, materials, and services converge, software is the thread that holds them together. Robots can be copied and services replicated, but an integrated system from design to construction is far harder to match.
