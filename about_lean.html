My thoughts on product

A few things I've come to believe

Velocity alone never builds a great product

Moving fast is frequently necessary but not sufficient, and it can mean going the wrong way faster. Great teams ship consistently, but hold a quality standard they won't drop under pressure, and use short experiments as a way to validate direction, but never as a substitute for committing to a clear goal.

Trust past behaviors

While people can very clearly articulate what they think they want or will do, past behaviors are much better predictors of how they make decisions. It's more effective to watch what people actually do, and find examples of how they've behaved in similar situations, than to request they anticipate their future reactions.

Each team has secret superpowers

It pays off to spend time discovering what teams are genuinely best at and energized by, and build the work around those strengths. It makes for happier teams and a truer read of actual capabilities and identification of gaps, rather than trying to force-fit people into rigid roles that sound good on paper but rarely describe real humans.

Flex the method to reality

No single discovery or development process fits every product or organization, and every team and stack has its own constraints. I'd rather customize an approach for the specific outcome desired and resources available than rely on a popular one that doesn't fit the team or culture.

How I work with AI

AI as a force multiplier, not a substitute for expertise

I use AI heavily and deliberately, but always on top of real product judgment and craft. On its own it drifts, and it can lull you into false confidence if you don't have the underlying expertise to know where it's likely to go wrong. What makes it powerful is clear direction, trusted data, consistent rules, and the ability to identify the failure modes.

At ICON, I used Claude regularly to generate and pressure-test design concepts, comb through lengthy research for undiscovered patterns, manage a design system at scale, and build working prototypes that communicated vision far faster than mockups. When missing team capacity was impacting the team, I built an automated product-management system that processed dozens of meeting transcripts a week, tracked my roadmap against Jira ticket status, and suggested sprint plans and generated progress reports.

We have a clear opportunity to rethink the work humans are best at and most satisfied by, while making the redundant work we've always done higher-quality, more consistent, and mind-blowingly fast. Layered on real expertise, it can surface new connections we couldn't see before. Done right, it lets us spend more time with our customers, not less, drawing out the value they actually need.
