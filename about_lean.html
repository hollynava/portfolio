About

I flex to what the problem and the team need.

I'm a design-rooted product leader who creates clarity in complex spaces, most often where the physical and digital meet. I'm most useful on the problems that are fragmented and ambiguous, where the tidy version on paper doesn't match how things actually work.

My instinct is to architect from what's actually there: the real tools, the real data, and the real people, rather than from what a plan says should exist. I flex to the range each problem needs, from setting an ecosystem's strategy to designing the exact interface someone uses in the field, and to the team, by meeting people where their real strengths are. And I stay hands-on throughout: I dig in and do the work myself, close to the craft rather than directing from a distance.

I do my best work with real authority and direct lines to the people I'm building for: users and decision-makers within reach, low silos, and a mission that actually matters. That's when the range pays off.

Hot Takes

A few things I've come to believe

Flex the method, hold the vision

No single process fits every product; every team and stack has its own constraints. I'd rather find or shape the right tool for the specific challenge and outcome than force-fit a familiar one to check a box.

Velocity alone never builds a great product

Moving fast is necessary but not sufficient, and it can just mean going the wrong way faster. Great teams ship quickly, hold a standard they won't drop, and treat short experiments as a way to find direction, never a substitute for a clear vision.

Find the team's true superpowers instead of force-fitting

I start from what people are genuinely best at and energized by, and build the work around that. It makes for happier teams and a truer read of what a team can actually do, not just what it should on paper.

Specialists are interventions; experienced generalists steer the ship

Depth matters most at key moments, to set taste and rigor. Between them, a design-rooted generalist holds direction and knows when to bring depth back in, at the right time and for the right duration, before the product stalls.

AI as a multiplier, not an imposter

AI is powerful layered on real expertise and dangerous as a quick replacement for it. Left alone it can lull you into false confidence, so you need people who know where it goes wrong steering and validating it.

How I work with AI

AI is as good as the direction you give it

I use AI heavily and deliberately, but always on top of real product judgment and craft. On its own it drifts, and it can lull you into false confidence if you don't know where it's likely to go wrong; what makes it reliable is clear direction, consistent rules, and a human who can spot the failure modes. With that in place it's genuinely powerful, whether it's generating and pressure-testing design concepts, combing through lengthy research for patterns easy to miss, managing a design system at scale, or building working prototypes that communicate a vision faster than clickable mockups. At ICON I built a Claude-based product-management system that processed dozens of meeting transcripts a week and tracked my roadmap to suggest sprint plans, filling missing team capacity during a hard phase.

We have a clear opportunity to rethink the work humans are best at and most satisfied by, while making the redundant work we've always done higher-quality, more consistent, and mind-blowingly fast. Layered on real expertise, it can surface new connections buried in vast amounts of seemingly disparate information and trends. Done right, it lets us spend more time with our customers, not less, drawing out the value they actually need.
