In a catch-up with a friend who works at a podcast advertising and distribution platform, I got intrigued by a discussion about their current challenges and a potential opportunity for growth: building their own podcast player. I set out to design the product that puts listeners, creators, and advertisers on the same side, then took it the whole distance myself: from an Amazon-style press release and product, through market and business strategy, to a working, deployed app. I built the entire thing within one week, using Claude as a force multiplier, as a sample of how I work.

Nobody aligns the listener, the creator, and the ad

Every major podcast app fails listeners somewhere. Apple's playback and queue are messy, Spotify buries podcasts and paywalls downloads, YouTube has no real "continue listening" and paywalls background audio. Each is optimizing for a business model, an app store, a music subscription, an ad exchange, that points away from the listener.

Underneath that, the economics are misaligned. Host-read ads are the format listeners tolerate and creators depend on, but they have always been hard to measure, so the money keeps drifting toward formats that serve the platform instead of the show. I wanted to design the product where what delights a listener and what grows a creator's business finally point the same direction, as well as tailoring to the company's business needs.

Decided in writing, then built it for real

Researched existing products

Analyzed popular podcast apps, digging into customer reviews and comparing feature sets and gaps. I also studied a major podcast hosting and advertising platform's business case and reviews for core needs and current gaps. (Based only on publicly available information, with no affiliation.)

Crafted the product vision

I wrote an Amazon-style PR-FAQ as a real press article to force clarity on the value before designing a single screen, scoping to two in-app customers, listeners and creators, with advertiser value flowing through existing ad products rather than a new console. I also created a basic PRD and feature descriptions.

Designed the signature moves

A simple design system, components, and brand look and feel, then the primary screens: integrated creator views, listener-adjustable and transparent recommendation controls, clear resume-episode and playlist management, and persistent access to offers.

Built it for real

A working progressive web app pulling live RSS feeds: real playback, queue, continue-listening, offline and installable, and a Shorts clip feed, plus simulated offer cards and creator views.

A usable product, not a mockup

OpenSignal carries the "listeners and creators on the same wavelength" thesis from end to end: from market analysis and PR through to a deployed app you can actually use. Every control is real and interactive. It's the single clearest proof of how I work, start from the customer outcome, make the business case unmistakable, then ship something real that can be validated.
