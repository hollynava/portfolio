The problem

A beta shipped ahead of the trust it needed

Vitruvius was ICON's internal generative-AI product: describe your dream home in natural language, and an LLM-based system returns interior and exterior renderings and a simple floor plan. It was pushed to a public beta for a marketing moment before the experience was ready.

In use, the seams showed. Floor plans didn't match the 3D imagery, some outputs were architecturally nonsensical, and navigation confused non-technical users. For a tool asking people to imagine their actual home, that gap between what the AI promised and what it delivered was eroding trust fast.

What I did

Steadied the experience, then pointed it somewhere real

Tested the beta live

I ran moderated usability sessions across homeowners, builders, and industry-adjacent users, then delivered findings with clear severity ratings and primary- and secondary-customer clarity, so the team knew what to fix first and for whom.

Fixed the immediate experience

I redesigned the core AI-interaction and navigation patterns, simplified the key flows, and created a responsive and mobile approach, so the product people could already reach was less confusing and less brittle.

Defined the future product

I set a vision that moved Vitruvius from novelty toward a real tool: interactive 3D massing, editable floor plans, integrated documentation and BOMs, real material selection, and a tiered monetization strategy to support it.

Gave people more control of the outcome

Throughout, I framed where a person should hold the decisions that shape the result and where the AI should assist, so people had more control of the outcome and stepped into the loop at the moments that actually mattered, rather than the tool pretending it was further along than it was.

Outcome

Clarity in the gap between AI's promise and its reach

The work delivered immediate UX improvements for the live beta, a clear, prioritized path toward product maturity, and a documented strategy for how AI-human design could feel trustworthy and usable. Development was later paused over the real technical challenge of generating architecture-grade outputs, a candid lesson in the messy space where innovation meets reality, and exactly the kind of judgment call I want to be close to.
